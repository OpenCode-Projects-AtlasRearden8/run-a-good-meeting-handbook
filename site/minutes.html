Most minutes are unreadable because they try to be a transcript. Minutes are not a recording — they're a receipt. Read receipts, not diaries.

The secret: decide what you're keeping

Minutes serve exactly four questions. If a line of writing doesn't answer one of them, it doesn't belong:

  1. What did we decide? Clear enough that a member who missed the meeting knows what changed.
  2. What happens next? Action items with a name and a date ("Dana books the hall by Friday").
  3. What did we table? The parking lot, so nothing dies by forgetting.
  4. Who was there? Attendance, so future-you knows who was in the room when the vote happened.

Everything else — the spirited debate, the side jokes, the "so anyway, as I was saying" — is not minutes. It's memory. Let it stay in the room.

During the meeting: capture, don't compose

  • Take bullet points, not paragraphs. Minutes get composed after, in five quiet minutes.
  • Each time a decision happens, mark it out loud: "Decision: we'll spend up to $200 on venue." That becomes the read-back at wrap-up too.
  • Flag actions the moment someone says "I'll email her" — don't trust yourself to catch it later.
  • You don't need to identify every speaker. Only names matter for motions, votes, and action items.

After the meeting: the five-minute rule

Within a day (ideally within the hour), spend five minutes turning bullets into the template. Then send it once, to the same channel as the agenda — with a subject line that carries the headline: "Minutes — Purple Lens — Nov 4 (venue decided: $200 cap)" tells members whether they need to open it.

The crucial move: start the next meeting's agenda card from these minutes. "Old business" items come straight off the action list. That's the loop that makes minutes earn their keep.

Formatting rules

  • Bold the decisions. The eye should scan them in ten seconds.
  • One line per action, name first: "Dana — book hall — by Fri."
  • No walls of text. Over three sentences? Ask whether it answers one of the four questions.
  • Nothing sensitive gets characterized. Discipline, personnel, personal matters: keep to a factual minimum ("Executive session held; no action taken"). Minutes are records people may read years later, including outsiders. This isn't legal advice — just a plain, careful-observer rule.
Rotate minute-taking. Put "minutes: Dana" at the top of the agenda card so it's assigned before the meeting, not negotiated at the end. The same person doing it forever is how minutes become one person's private chore.

The template

Prints on one sheet. If a section is empty, delete it — blank scaffolding makes minutes look unfinished.

MINUTES — [GROUP NAME]
Date: [date]        Location/link: [where]
Facilitator: [name]  Minutes taken by: [name]

ATTENDEES
[present] · Absent: [names, or "—"]

DECISIONS MADE
- **[Decision 1 — stated so an absent member understands what changed]**
  (approved [X–Y] / by consensus / carried over from [date])
- **[Decision 2]**

ACTION ITEMS
- [Name] — [task] — due [date]
- [Name] — [task] — due [date]

TABLED / PARKING LOT
- [item] — [owner or "someday"]

NOTABLE DISCUSSION (2–4 bullets max, only if it aids future decisions)
- [the one fact or constraint people will need to remember]

NEXT MEETING
[date, time, place, who takes minutes]

(Financial note if applicable: balance as of [date]: [amount]; changes: [what and why].)

Filled-in sample (fictional club)

MINUTES — Purple Lens Photography Club
Date: Tue Nov 4, 2026        Location: Community Center Room 2 + Zoom
Facilitator: Maya            Minutes taken by: Dana

ATTENDEES
Maya, Dana, Priya, Jordan, Sam, Lori, Chen, Bea, Theo · Remote: Alex
Absent: Rosa (excused)

DECISIONS MADE
- **Spring fundraiser will be a print sale (not a photo walk), with a
  $200 cap on printing and materials.** (approved 7–2)
- **Sale date set for Sat Mar 14, contingent on hall availability.** (consensus)

ACTION ITEMS
- Dana — book hall for Mar 14 and confirm refund policy — due Fri Nov 6
- Priya — collect member print submissions (max 3 each) — due Dec 2
- Jordan — measure storage room for shelves — due Wed Nov 12
- Maya — draft fundraiser budget at the $200 cap — due Nov 18 meeting

TABLED / PARKING LOT
- Darkroom repaint — someday, needs paint estimate
- Gear-swap event idea — Sam — revisit in January

NOTABLE DISCUSSION
- Held indoors rather than a walk because last year's walk lost $40 to rain.
  Print sale priced at cost + 30% (see Maya's draft budget next time).

NEXT MEETING
Tue Nov 18, 7:00 PM, Room 2 · minutes: Jordan

FINANCIAL NOTE
Balance as of Nov 4: $1,860. Changes since Oct 21: −$150 darkroom supplies.
Fake details, real structure. The "Purple Lens" club is invented for the samples — swap in your own names and numbers.
