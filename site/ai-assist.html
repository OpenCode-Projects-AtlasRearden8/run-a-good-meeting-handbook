A Gentle AI Assist for Recaps

AI does the tidy-up labor. Humans keep the judgment.

This chapter is deliberately tool-agnostic: whatever AI assistant you use, the pattern below stays the same — AI is a helpful typist, not the president of your club.

The rule of thumbs

  1. Humans decide; AI tidies. Never let an AI output become a decision on its own — you review every line before anyone acts on it.
  2. Privacy first. Strip names (except action-item owners your group has already published), figures you're not comfortable sharing, and anything sensitive before pasting text into any tool. If your tool records audio, only record with everyone's clear okay, and check your group's bylaws.
  3. Verify easy things yourself. Dates, names, dollar amounts: ten seconds of checking beats an error living in your minutes for a year.

Use 1: Bullets into a readable recap

You took rough bullet notes during the meeting (see the minutes chapter). Paste them in with a prompt like this:

Here are rough meeting notes from our club meeting. Turn them into minutes
using this structure only — do not invent anything not in the notes, and if
something is unclear, write [CHECK: your best guess]. Keep it under one page.
Structure:
- Date & attendees
- Decisions (bold)
- Action items (name — task — due date)
- Tabled / parking lot
- Next meeting

Notes: [paste notes]

The phrases doing the real work are "do not invent anything" and "write [CHECK: your best guess]" — they turn a confident guesser into an honest assistant that flags holes instead of papering over them. Fill the [CHECK] items yourself from memory.

Use 2: Meeting notes into a friendly announcement

Minutes are for the record; announcements get read:

Write a 5-sentence email to club members based on these minutes: warm in
tone, front-loads the one big decision, ends with who needs to do what by
when. Keep our informal voice, no corporate phrases.

You proofread and sign it yourself. The content is your minutes; the assistant is doing layout.

Use 3: The decision history

Paste the last three meetings' minutes and ask:

List every open action item across these minutes, grouped by owner,
with the latest due date mentioned for each. Flag any that appear
twice with different dates.

That last flag catches the classic club bug: the same task carried forward with quietly drifting deadlines. AI is genuinely good at this boring cross-referencing — the best non-creative use on this list.

Use 4: Agenda help

At the start of the cycle: "Here are last month's minutes. Draft next meeting's agenda card: housekeeping, one main item, and questions to ask to close each open action." Treat the result as a first draft — your agenda, your judgment on what matters.

What not to hand to AI

  • Raw recordings of sensitive discussions — personnel, discipline, members' personal finances.
  • Voting records you intend to keep private — some orgs publish them, some don't; know which you are before pasting.
  • Anything where being wrong matters a lot — financial statements, legal notices, bylaws amendments. Draft by hand; use AI only to check tone or clarity.
AI makes recaps 80% faster because 80% of recap work is mechanical reorganization. The remaining 20% — judgment about what matters, what's fair, what's kind — is yours. And that's the good news: it stays that way.
