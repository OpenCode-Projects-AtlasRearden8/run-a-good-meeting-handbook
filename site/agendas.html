Agendas That Fit on a Card

A short, honest plan for what you'll actually talk about.

An agenda is not a document. It's a promise of what will happen, small enough that everyone actually looks at it. If it feels like homework, it's too long.

The card test

Imagine your agenda pencilled onto a 4×6 index card. If it doesn't fit — or you "need smaller handwriting" — it's a wish list, not an agenda. The structure below fixes that by design: everything ends up on one card.

The structure: three sections only

  1. Housekeeping (2 minutes) — roll call, last meeting's decisions recap, one-line treasurer/officer updates.
  2. The main thing (most of the time) — the one discussion the meeting exists for: a decision to make, a problem to solve, or a plan to build.
  3. Wrap-up (5 minutes) — read the decisions aloud, confirm who does what by when, pick the next meeting date.

If you find yourself with four main things, that's two meetings. Handle the smaller ones by email or quick messages, and protect the time for the one thing that really needs everyone together.

The five fields

Next to each item, note three things — this turns a topic list into a working tool:

  • Who leads it — one name per item.
  • What "done" looks like — usually "we decide" or "we agree to X."
  • A time limit — even a rough one. "Fundraiser — Priya — decide budget — 15 min."
Send the card 24 hours ahead wherever your group already communicates. At the start, read the purpose out loud in one sentence: "Tonight we pick the spring fundraiser and confirm the budget." That ten-second habit keeps drift in check better than any other technique.

The parking lot

Keep a running "parking lot" list for interesting tangents ("that reminds me of the storage room problem..."). Write them down, say "parking that, let's finish the decision", and add it to a future card. Nothing is lost — it's just rescheduled. This single habit fixes most "we ran out of time" meetings.

Common failure modes

  • The 40-item agenda. Ten items touched meaninglessly for two hours. Cut to three and actually finish them.
  • "Old business." Most old business is unrecorded decisions resurfacing. Good minutes fix this.
  • Discussion before the item is framed. Ask the leader to state the question first: "We need to pick between location A and B by tonight." Then talk.
  • Starting five minutes late. People learn within two meetings. Don't rewind for latecomers — the minutes recorded what they missed.

The template

This template is print-friendly — the whole card fits on one page of paper, or an index card if you're lucky.

MEETING CARD — [GROUP NAME]
[Date] · [Start time] · [Location / video link]
Purpose in one sentence: [Tonight/Today we ____ ]
Facilitator: [name]   Minutes: [name]   Timekeeper: [name or "chair"]

HOUSEKEEPING (5 min)
- [ ] Attendance & welcome new folks
- [ ] Recap of last meeting's decisions (minutes: [link or read-aloud])
- [ ] Quick reports (treasurer, officers) — 1 min each, updates only

THE MAIN THING ([__] min)
[ ] [Item 1 title] — [who leads] — [decide / discuss / plan] — [time]
    Question to answer: [Exactly what are we deciding or solving?]

WRAP-UP (5 min)
- [ ] Read back: decisions + who does what by when
- [ ] Parking lot review (add new items here, don't debate them)
- [ ] Next meeting date & who's on the card

PARKING LOT (from last time)
- [item] — [owner or "someday"]

BEFORE YOU SEND: Does the "main thing" have a deadline tonight?
Anything not needed by everyone? → handle by email/chat instead.

Filled-in sample (fictional club)

MEETING CARD — PURPLE LENS PHOTOGRAPHY CLUB
Tue Nov 4 · 7:00 PM · Community Center Room 2 (Zoom: [link])
Purpose: tonight we pick the spring fundraiser and cap its budget.
Facilitator: Maya   Minutes: Dana   Timekeeper: Priya

HOUSEKEEPING (5 min)
- [x] Attendance: 9 present, 1 remote
- [x] Last meeting recap: darkroom supplies approved ($150)
- [x] Treasurer: $1,860 balance; dues current

THE MAIN THING (30 min)
[x] Spring fundraiser — Priya — DECIDE — 30 min
    Question: print sale or photo walk? Budget cap how much?

WRAP-UP (5 min)
- [x] Read back: print sale chosen; $200 cap on materials; Dana books table
- [x] Parking: darkroom repaint (someday), gear-swap idea (Sam)
- [x] Next: Nov 18, same room, minutes: Jordan

PARKING LOT (from last time)
- Storage room shelves — Jordan — check measurements by Nov 12
Fake details, real structure. The "Purple Lens" club is invented for the samples — swap in your own names and numbers.
