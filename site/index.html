Meetings that should take 30 minutes keep taking 90, and everyone shrugs. This short handbook is for the people who run five-to-fifteen-person meetings — a small business, a club, a committee, a co-op, a church group — and just want them to be short, kind, and useful.

Almost every meeting problem traces back to one thing: no one said out loud what the meeting was for.

The four chapters

  • 01Agendas that fit on a card Three sections, five fields, a parking lot — one index card, zero homework.
  • 02Minutes people actually read Minutes are a receipt, not a transcript: decisions, actions, tabled items, done.
  • 03Keeping the meeting on track (kindly) Seven situations, word-for-word lines that redirect without embarrassing anyone.
  • 04A gentle AI assist for recaps Copy-paste prompts for clean recaps — humans decide, AI tidies, privacy first.

Grab the templates

Each page prints on one or two sheets — or onto an actual index card if your printer cooperates.

The one rule of this handbook: before anything else, every meeting gets a stated purpose ("decide X", "update on Y") and a named person who wraps it up. Everything else is detail around that habit.
This handbook is meeting craft, not professional advice. It layers on top of Robert's Rules if your organization requires them; it isn't legal, financial, or medical guidance.
